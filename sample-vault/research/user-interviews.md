# User interview synthesis

Eight interviews in April 2026, 35 minutes each. Five knowledge workers, two
writers, one graduate student. All of them keep notes daily and all of them have
tried at least one AI note tool.

## Finding 1: The folder is the product

Seven of eight people already keep notes in plain files or an app that exports
them. Nobody wants another silo. The moment we said "point it at a folder you
already have", the conversation changed from evaluation to adoption.

> "I have eleven years of notes in markdown. I'm not moving them into your
> database so a chatbot can read them." — P3, product manager

## Finding 2: Privacy is specific, not abstract

People did not object to AI in principle. They objected to particular files
leaving: journals, health notes, anything about their children, drafts of things
they had not shipped. When we showed the label system, every participant asked
whether they could mark a single note private rather than the whole vault.

> "I'd use it for meeting notes tomorrow. I would never put my journal in it." — P1

## Finding 3: Price expectations cluster at ten

When asked what they would pay, answers ranged from "nothing, I'd use the free
version" to 15 EUR. Four people landed at or near 10 EUR per month for a personal
tool. Two said they would pay more only if sharing with a collaborator worked well.

## Finding 4: Onboarding is the risk, not the AI

Five participants could not explain what "on-device" meant before we said it out
loud. After one sentence ("the model runs inside your browser, nothing is
uploaded"), all five could repeat it correctly. The label has to be visible in the
product, not buried in a settings page.

## What this changes

- Lead with the folder, not the model.
- Make privacy per-note by default, with a clear marker in the note header.
- Price at 6 EUR for solo, keep the free tier genuinely useful.
- Put the "what leaves your device" panel in the demo video; it tested extremely well.

## Quotes worth asking permission for

- "It's the first AI tool that didn't ask me to trust it." — P5
- "Show me the payload and I'll decide." — P7
